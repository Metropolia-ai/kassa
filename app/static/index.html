How to test Kassa

Kassa is an AI bookkeeper for a small company. It reads supplier invoices, pays the ones that pass the payment policy through PayPal Payouts, holds the rest for a person, and bills customers through PayPal Invoicing. Everything runs in the PayPal sandbox: no real money moves.

  1. Load sample inbox. The model reads five supplier invoices. Two are approved on their own because the suppliers are trusted and the amounts are under the limit. Two wait under “Needs you”. One is caught as a duplicate.
  2. Look at the €4,980 bill from “Finance Dept”. The table and the queue mark it in red. It is a typical invoice scam: urgency, secrecy, a new account, a sender domain that imitates the company. Kassa lists the signals and holds it even though nothing technical is wrong. Reject it, or approve it to see what happens.
  3. Approve Nordic Voice Studio, tick “Trust this supplier”, then press Pay approved bills. All approved bills go to PayPal Payouts. Their stamp turns from Sent to Paid when PayPal confirms; “Check PayPal status” asks PayPal right away.
  4. Open “Invoices sent” and press Send sample invoices. Three customer invoices go out through PayPal. “Open” shows the page the customer gets.
  5. Ask Kassa on the right, for example “What is waiting for me and why?” or “Invoice Acme Oy 1500 EUR for the pilot, due in 14 days”. The agent works through tools. It can pay what is approved, but it cannot approve a payment itself.
  6. Check “Activity”. Every action by Kassa or a person is recorded with the reason given at the time, including refusals.

Try changing “Pays on its own up to” in the header and loading the inbox again. “Start over” clears all demo data. Click any row to see the full record and the original invoice text.
